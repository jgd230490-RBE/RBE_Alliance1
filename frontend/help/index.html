User guide v1.0 · 10 September 2026 · back to the app · public map

1. Welcome

RBE Alliance 1 Logistics is the shared planning tool for road haulage on the Rail Baltica Estonia Alliance 1 programme. Every IPT forecasts its material movements into it, planners approve them, and the week's haulage commitment — how much moves on which route, on which day, with how many trucks — comes out of it. A public map shows anyone where the hauls run, what is in the way on the roads, and which works package owns each stretch of the alignment.

This guide is for the people who use the platform: IPT submitters, planners and anyone who reads the public map. It explains each screen, what to type, what the numbers mean and where they come from. It deliberately leaves out internal names — the technical guide has those.

Access codes are shared per role and checked by the server on every request. Your IPT code shows you only your IPT's lines, wherever you look. Per-person logins with passwords are planned as the next security phase; until then treat your code as you would a shared key.

1.3 How the numbers are built — in one picture

Everything you type is a quantity per month on one route for one material and one vehicle type. The platform converts it to tonnes using the material's density, to trips using the vehicle's payload, and to vehicles using how many trips one truck can make in a 10-hour shift on that route's measured cycle. Distances and drive times come from truck routing for the exact vehicle, out laden and back empty. Section 9 explains each step and what "planning figure" means.

2. Getting started

2.1 Signing in

  • Open the site address. The public map is shown behind a Staff sign in button at the top right.
  • Type your name and your access code. Your name is stamped on the lines you submit and the weeks you confirm, so use the name your colleagues know.
  • The code is kept for this browser tab only; closing the tab signs you out. Your name and your last page are remembered on this computer.
  • If the platform says the code is not recognised, check for a trailing space and that you have the current code — codes are rotated by the planners.

2.2 Finding your way around

The left rail is grouped: Plan (Dashboard, Submit forecast, Forecasts), Track (Look-ahead), Data (Locations, Routes, Zones, Config — planners and admins only) and Map (the public map inside the app). The rail can be collapsed to icons with the button at its foot; it remembers the page you were on. The Forecasts entry carries a badge with the number of lines waiting for approval when you are a planner.

  • Vehicle names can be shown in English trade names or as EU vehicle categories — the EN / EU / EE toggle in the header changes the label only; nothing stored changes. Estonian labels are not filled in yet and fall back to the EU string with a star.
  • The header shows your role and, for an IPT code, your IPT. Sign out is in the header.
  • The app works in a desktop browser at 1440 px wide or more; the staff app has no phone layout. The public map does.

3. The Dashboard

The Dashboard is the strategic view of the forecast. Filter by status (Approved, Pending or all), unit, material, vehicle and month range. The six tiles read Total material, Total trips, Truck-km, CO₂e, Intensity and Peak month; below them are charts for material demand over time, vehicle trips over time, carbon over time, the material split and the top routes by tonnage, then a per-route table (Route planning detail) with the cycle time, trips per day and vehicles for each route and vehicle.

  • Truck-km, CO₂e and vehicles use the routed distance and cycle for each line's route and vehicle. A route that is not yet baked for that vehicle shows "not baked" rather than a figure.
  • Vehicles are trucks, not trips: peak demand per day divided by what one truck can physically do in a shift. Hover the tile for the rule.
  • The Dashboard does not calculate anything of its own; it reads the same figures the Look-ahead and the public map use, so the three cannot disagree.

Stock held lists every stockpile for this month and next: the balance at the end of each week, what came in, what went out, and the remaining capacity — or OVER in red when a stockpile's recorded capacity is exceeded. "no cap" means nobody has recorded a capacity for it. This panel is read-only; consumption is typed on the Look-ahead's Account view (§6.4).

4.1 The totals strip

As you type, the strip at the foot of the form updates: the total, the equivalent tonnes, average and peak movements per day (the month's loads divided by 22 working days), and vehicles needed (average and peak) — the movements per day divided by the trips one truck can make in a shift on this route with this vehicle. That last figure needs the route to be baked for the vehicle; if it is not, the strip says "not baked" and nothing is estimated in its place. Tell a planner: baking is a one-click action on the Routes page.

4.2 Warnings you may see

  • Seasonal restriction — a quantity in April or May on a 44-tonne artic shows the spring-thaw warning (axle-weight limits are likely on secondary roads). It warns; it does not block or re-route. Winter shows a message only.
  • Not baked — the route has no measured geometry for this vehicle yet. You can still submit; the line simply carries no distance or vehicle figures until a planner bakes it.
  • Caution on save — another line already exists for this route and month (for example a second discipline). Both stay; the caution makes sure you know.

4.3 Saving, editing and withdrawing

Save writes every month in the range; a cell left at 0 removes that month. The line appears on the Forecasts page as Pending until a planner approves it. To change it later, open the line from the Forecasts page with Edit — the whole year range reopens in this form. Withdraw removes the whole line across every year it covers; the confirmation tells you the span and the number of months.

5. The Forecasts page

Every forecast line you are allowed to see, grouped per route, discipline and work section across every year it covers. Filter by status (the count chips are clickable), IPT, discipline, "mine only" and free text. Export CSV downloads what is on screen.

5.1 For planners: approving and rejecting

  • Approve and Reject act on the line (route, discipline and section), not on the whole route. A rejection needs a reason — the form refuses an empty one — and the reason is shown to the submitter.
  • Approving a month creates its weeks in the Look-ahead immediately and puts the line on the public map. Reopening or rejecting an approved month later does not delete weeks that already carry typed actuals; they simply stop refreshing and show "month is now Pending".
  • Approval does not check that the route is baked for the vehicle. The Look-ahead will flag an unbaked line (UNBAKED) and show trips only. Bake before you approve, or straight after.

6. The Look-ahead

The Look-ahead turns an approved monthly forecast into a weekly plan, and the week that contains today into a daily commitment sheet for hauliers. It has three views, switched with the pills under the page title: Account (last week — what actually happened), Commit (this week, or next week — the plan by day) and Horizon (the coming weeks as totals). It opens on Commit.

6.1 How weeks work

  • A week runs Monday to Sunday and belongs to the month that holds its Thursday — the ISO numbering on Estonian calendars. A month therefore has four or five weeks; September 2026 has four, October and December five.
  • An approved month's quantity is split equally over its weeks, and each week equally over Monday to Friday. Saturday and Sunday default to zero.
  • Anything you type over — a week or a day — stops following the split and is marked as typed. If the month is later re-approved with a different quantity, typed weeks keep their values and show PARENT CHANGED rather than silently moving.

6.2 The weekly rhythm

6.3 Commit view — this week by day

The header shows the week's dates. The KPI strip reads: planned this week (tonnes), trips, vehicles per day at the peak, t·km, planned € (or "no rate typed"), last week's shortage, and last week delivered: n of m lines — a line counts as delivered when the actual reached 98 % of the plan.

The clash rail under the strip lists flags for the week — up to three, then "+N more". Flags never block anything; they tell you what to look at (§10 lists them all). The most important for an IPT is IPT SHARE: another IPT is on the same route, or the same origin and destination, on the same day. You are told you share it without seeing the other IPT's quantities.

Each row of the grid is one line: origin → destination, an IPT chip, a work-section chip, the material and the vehicle. The Monday–Friday cells show the quantity and, beneath it, trips · vehicles for that day — for example 17 tr · 3 veh. Today's column is tinted.

  • Click a row to see km per trip, km per day, km per vehicle, t·km, the cycle time and the € for the line — or "rate not set" if no rate has been typed on the route.
  • Type over a day when the site's plan differs from the even split. The cell shows a typed marker and the day no longer follows the week total. If the days then no longer add up to the week, the line shows DAYS ≠ WEEK — a flag, not an error; you can still confirm.
  • Unbaked line — a route with no measured geometry for the vehicle shows trips but veh —, and the flag UNBAKED. Nothing is estimated in its place; ask a planner to bake the route.
  • The week map under the grid draws this week's lines in their IPT colour, origin dot navy and destination dot red; hover a line for its quantity and trips. A line with no geometry is listed under the map rather than drawn as a straight line.

Confirming the week

Confirm week (the green button, top right) is one act for the whole week: every planned cell locks, actuals stay typeable, and the sheet becomes the commitment. The dialog names the week and the number of lines and has four free-text notes — weather, wetness, traffic, other — for the record. Confirm remaining lines appears when some lines are already confirmed. A confirmed week can be re-opened to change the plan; nothing is deleted.

This week / next week

On Thursday you may want to commit next week while this week is still running. Switch the pill to Commit · next week: its days are created on demand and can be typed and confirmed in the same way. A shortfall spread on Thursday with a partial actual and again on Friday with the full figure carries only the difference — the platform never counts the same shortfall twice.

Exporting for hauliers

  • Export XLSX — a workbook with one row per line per day (quantity, tonnes, trips, vehicles, km, t·km, €, cycle and its source, day status), a Clashes sheet and an About sheet. Weekdays only.
  • PDF — the one-week commitment sheet: landscape, a route map on the first page, one row per line sorted by origin, Monday to Friday columns with quantity and trips tr · veh, origin and destination with coordinates, the week total, km per trip and € where a rate is typed, then the flags and the footer caveats. It says CONFIRMED only when every line is. It is a commitment sheet, not a delivery note: payloads are planning figures, not plated weights, and km come from truck routing.
  • If the PDF's map is a drawn schematic rather than a photo map, the caption says the map service could not be reached; the sheet is otherwise identical.

6.4 Account view — what actually happened last week

Account shows last week per line: planned, an actual box, the variance (green when over-delivered, red when short; an empty actual means "not reported", never zero), an actual € box for the invoiced or agreed cost of the week, a note, and the status of the shortfall — held, carried or remaining.

  • Type the actual quantity as it was delivered. Saving an actual never changes next week's plan by itself.
  • spread on this week carries a shortfall into the commit week, spread evenly over the remaining weekdays from today. Press it once the figure is known; pressing it again after a correction carries only the difference. It refuses if the target week is already confirmed — re-open the week first.
  • Actual € is the first place the platform records money spent rather than planned; the KPI strip compares it with the planned € where rates exist.
  • The Stockpiles block at the foot lists each stockpile for the account week: opening balance, inbound (from the typed actuals on routes delivering there — a route nobody reported counts as nothing), an out box for what was consumed, the closing balance and the remaining capacity. Type the consumption once a week; the Dashboard's Stock held panel and the public map's stockpile gauges read it.

6.5 Horizon view

Horizon shows week totals for this month and the next — four or five columns per month, each headed with its Monday–Sunday dates — with the commit week highlighted. Nothing is confirmed here; it is the look-ahead in the ordinary sense: what is coming, so make-ready and early warning can happen.

Good habits

Open the Look-ahead before a meeting: the page reads a lot of data and can take several seconds to load on first open.

Type actuals on Monday, confirm the week once the grid is right, export the PDF for hauliers, and use the next-week switch on Thursday.

Read the clash rail before confirming. A ROUTE CAP or STOCKPILE OVER flag will not stop you — it is a planner's call.

7. Data pages (planners and admins)

Locations, Routes and Zones share one map; switching between them keeps your view. Writes on these pages need the admin token typed into the box on the page (it is remembered in this browser). Paste it into the box, never into the address bar.

7.1 Locations

  • Add a location by clicking on the map in add mode, then complete the form: name, type (Quarry, Port, Compound, Site, Railhead, Stockpile, Other), what it supplies and receives (material categories — a route can only be created between an origin that supplies and a destination that receives the same category), operator and detail.
  • Drag a marker to move a location; every route using it is re-routed automatically.
  • Gates — a location can have several. Each has a direction (access, egress or both), a default flag, and two planning times: induction (safety briefing, applied once per arrival at that gate) and gate-to-face travel (ignored on routes that use a drawn haul road, which carries its own time). Routes start and end at gates, not at the site marker. A route can name a specific gate at either end; otherwise the location's default gate is used. A deactivated gate that a route still names stops that route from routing until the route is re-pointed.
  • Stockpile capacity — for stockpiles and other storage, record the capacity (tonnes or m³) and the opening quantity. Without a capacity a stockpile can never show as over; the map draws it with a dashed outline to say so.
  • Deleting a location cascades to its routes; the confirmation says what goes.

7.2 Routes

  • Create a route from an origin and destination (only valid pairs are offered; the id is assigned as R001, R002 …). Set the material category and, if the route belongs to one IPT, the IPT.
  • Bake — pressing Bake asks the routing service for the route as driven by each ticked vehicle: out laden and back empty, both as a truck of that weight, height and axle count. "Baked" is per vehicle; the chips show fully baked (green) or outbound-only (amber). Bake all runs the whole network in batches; the cost estimate counts calls, and a route with a temporary haul road costs more.
  • Route analysis row — km and drive time per leg, turnaround, cycle time, trips per day, tonnes per day and per month for each vehicle. A ‡ marks a cycle that includes a gate induction figure or an estimated return leg.
  • Alternatives — the routing service returns up to three options; the extra ones are shown when a route is selected. Use this option promotes an alternative to be the primary. A promotion is lost when the route is next baked (a moved location, an edited endpoint, a zone change, a vehicle change or Bake all).
  • Vehicles — tick or untick the vehicles a route is baked for. The confirmation names the forecast lines that use each vehicle and warns when the last vehicle would go.
  • Edit origin, destination, material or IPT in place. The impact dialog shows what will change — forecasts and weeks are kept; moving an endpoint clears the cached geometry and that end's gate, and the affected vehicles are re-baked.
  • Planning · Look-ahead block — the optional figures the Look-ahead prices and checks against: max vehicles per day (a planning cap that raises ROUTE CAP, never a block), € per load, € per tonne, € per km (the three add up — type what the haulier actually quotes, typically a base per load plus a per-km rate), and the km basis for €/km and t·km (round trip, the default, or loaded leg only). Nothing is pre-filled; a route reads "rate not set" until you type one.
  • Road restrictions (Tark Tee) — check now runs the route against the Transport Administration's live restriction data in the background and stores the result with its time; the Look-ahead and the exports show the hits and their age. A re-bake clears the stored check.
  • Diagnostics card — "Check factors" confirms every vehicle is matched to its configuration entry; "Probe" runs a live routing call for one route and shows what the service returned.

7.3 Zones and temporary haul roads

  • Disruption zone — draw a polygon on the map, give it a name, a kind (closure, weight limit, works, other), an optional date window and whether it affects routing. When it does, its bounding box is sent to the routing service as an area to avoid, every route crossing it is re-routed, and it draws red on both maps; otherwise it is drawn grey for information. The panel prints the exact box that will be sent before you save. Because the service takes boxes, not shapes, a long diagonal zone blocks more than it should — draw several small ones. Deleting a zone does not put the old route back; deactivating is usually the better choice.
  • Temporary haul road — draw a line, give it an assigned speed, and attach it to routes from the route's expanded row (in order, if a route uses more than one). The routing service does not know a road cut last week exists; the platform routes to the road's end, uses your line at your speed, and routes on from the other end. The service's own time is kept alongside so the change is auditable. A road with no speed adds distance but no time and is flagged.
  • Zones and haul roads change routes, not forecasts: an approved forecast is not re-warned when its route gets longer. Look at the Dashboard or the Look-ahead after a change.

7.4 Config

Config holds every number the platform calculates from, on five tabs: Vehicles (gross weight, payload, kg CO₂e per km, load and unload minutes, routing dimensions, labels), Materials (density, default unit, which vehicles are allowed), Planning (working days per month, shift hours per day, fallback load and unload minutes), Seasonal restrictions (month windows, restricted vehicles, the message shown) and Everything (JSON) for anything the forms do not cover. Save applies the whole document, everywhere, for everyone, immediately; Reset to file restores the defaults shipped with the software. The page says whether the live configuration currently differs from those defaults.

Config refuses some edits, deliberately

You cannot remove a vehicle that has baked routes (every figure on those routes would silently fall to a hidden default and read the same), set a gross weight below a payload, name a vehicle that does not exist in a material category or a seasonal window, or remove the default routing vehicle. The error lists the problems; nothing is stored until they are fixed.

8.2 Filters, basemaps and overlays

  • Route filtering — origin, destination, discipline and sector IPT. The filters apply to routes, the timeline callouts and the KPI panel. Filter to your IPT and the timeline shows your traffic only. Reset filters clears them.
  • Basemap — Light (default), Standard (3D), Dark, Satellite (Mapbox) and the Estonia orthophoto (Maa-amet). Every overlay survives a basemap switch. 3D buildings are available on the vector basemaps only.
  • Overlays — Rail alignment (by IPT) with its legend; Work section boundaries; Alignment (survey, continuous) — the raw survey with every track type, off by default; Chainage markers (thinned by zoom so the corridor is not a smear); Sites & assets with its legend; 3D buildings; Site gates; Disruption zones; Rail network (EVR).
  • Haul routes — Laden (out), Empty (return), Temporary haul, Alternative routes.
  • A red banner above the IPT legend means the map's overlay files are out of step after an update — hard-refresh the page.

8.3 Road restrictions

Tick a layer under Road restrictions to draw it; the value (for example a 3.2 m height limit) is labelled from zoom 11. Click a restriction near a route for its details and the verdict against the vehicle — "3.2 m limit, vehicle 4.0 m, EXCEEDS by 0.8 m" — for mass, height and width. Weak bridges carry a load *class* rather than a tonnage, so their verdict is "unknown" with the class quoted. The data is live from tarktee.ee and refreshes itself every 30 minutes; only restrictions currently in force are shown. It is advisory: routes are not re-planned around it.

8.4 The forecast timeline and KPI panel

Under Forecast traffic volume, choose a month range and press ▶ Play forecast timeline. The map steps month by month through the approved forecasts: routes carrying volume are drawn, the sites in use get a white ring while everything else dims, small callouts name the busiest origins and destinations, and the KPI panel at the top left reads the month — Movements / day, Vehicles needed, Material / day and Active routes. Use the switcher under the cards to cut the month by Discipline, IPT or Work section. ◀ and ▶▶ step a month; × closes the timeline. Forecast detail table opens the month-by-month matrix.

  • Vehicles needed is the sum, line by line, of movements per day divided by what one truck can do on that route's cycle — a route that is not baked is counted as unknown and the label says how many lines are unbaked. An empty month shows dashes, not zero.
  • At a wide zoom a busy month shows fewer callouts than there are sites: labels that would overlap are dropped, busiest first. Zoom in and they come back.
  • The timeline plays approved monthly forecasts. It is a plan, not a live feed.

8.5 Street View

Click a site mark and press Street View for the nearest imagery. "No Street View here" means none exists within 50 m; the map checks for imagery before it fetches a picture, so nothing is spent on empty lookups.

9. Understanding the numbers

Trips (movements, loads)tonnes ÷ the vehicle's payload, rounded up. One trip is one loaded run.The payload is a typical carrying limit, not a legal maximum.

What "planning figure" means

Every quantity on a sheet is derived from typed forecasts and configured assumptions, not weighed loads or telematics. The supplier PDF says so in its footer: payloads are planning figures, not plated weights; km come from the routing service. Use the figures to plan fleets and spot clashes; do not present them as a delivery record.

10. Flags, chips and warnings

10.1 The Look-ahead clash rail

FlagIt meansWhat to do
SHORTAGELast week delivered less than planned and the shortfall has not been carried forward.On Account, press "spread on this week" — or leave it if the shortfall is not to be recovered.
DAYS ≠ WEEKThe typed days no longer add up to the week's plan.Adjust a day, or accept it — you can still confirm; the export prints both numbers.
STOCKPILE OVERThe destination stockpile's forecast balance exceeds its recorded capacity.Check the Account stock block; type consumption; reschedule inbound.
ROUTE CAPMore vehicles on this route on a day than the cap typed on the route — counting every IPT.A planner's call: move a day, raise the cap, or accept.
IPT SHAREAnother IPT is on the same route (or the same origin and destination, or the same haul road) on the same day.Coordinate with the other IPT; the platform does not show you their quantities.
TARK TEEA road restriction on the route's baked geometry, from the stored check; or the check is unavailable or has never run.Read the hit on the Routes page; press "check now" if it is stale.
UNBAKEDThe route has no measured geometry for this line's vehicle: trips only, no vehicles, km or t·km.Ask a planner to bake the route for that vehicle.
PARENT CHANGEDThe month's approved quantity changed after this week was typed; the week was deliberately not refreshed.Re-type the week if the new quantity should flow through.

10.2 Chips and marks

  • IPT chip and WS chip on every Look-ahead row and Forecasts line — the IPT chip uses the same six colours as the map's alignment.
  • Typed marker on a day cell — this day no longer follows the week split.
  • ‡ on a cycle — an estimated part (return leg not routed, or a gate induction figure).
  • "no IPT" on a line — an older line no IPT has claimed yet; planners only.
  • Red "not baked" on the Submit form and Dashboard — no geometry for the vehicle.

10.3 The public map's warning levels

When the timeline is playing, a stack above it can show up to three warnings for the month on screen: Clash — a stockpile past its capacity; Warning — a road restriction that a visible route's vehicle exceeds; Caution — a seasonal window (spring thaw) matching the month and vehicle. Each can be dismissed for that month. If the stack stays empty for a month you would expect to show something, the sources may not have loaded; reload the page.

Appendix A — Methods and assumptions

This annex states the data sources and assumptions behind the figures, for anyone who has to quote or defend a number. It contains no system internals.

A.1 Data sources

SourceUsed forLimits to know
HERE Routing (truck profile)Every route's geometry, distance and drive time — out laden at gross weight, back at empty weight, routed separately with the vehicle's height, width, length and axle count.Routes follow HERE's road graph, which can lag a recent road realignment on the programme; a route that follows an old road across a field is HERE's geometry, not a data-entry error. Drive times can vary slightly between calls; the km and cycle figures are planning figures.
Estonian Transport Administration (Tark Tee)Weak bridges, mass / height / width limits, traffic restrictions and diversions on the public map and in route checks.Advisory only — routes are not re-planned around it. Weak-bridge load classes are shown as given, without a verdict. Refreshed at least every 30 minutes; the authority's own update cadence is not published.
Surveyed rail alignmentThe corridor line coloured by IPT, the work-section boundaries and the chainage markers.About 60 km of the corridor has no surveyed track in the file; those stretches are bridged and labelled as not measured. The work-section boundary near WS2/WS3 is approximate.
Existing rail network (EVR)The reference corridor for railheads.Provisional reference geometry (Natural Earth), several km out at Lelle. Labelled provisional on the map.
Google Street ViewThe site thumbnail in the popup.Only where imagery exists within 50 m.
Mapbox and Maa-ametBasemaps and the PDF's route map.Presentation only.

A.3 What the platform does not do

  • It is not real-time and holds no vehicle positions; the timeline plays approved forecasts.
  • It has no time-of-day model: no delivery windows, gate booking or queueing. A daily route cap is compared with an average, not a schedule.
  • It does not enforce anything against a planner's judgement: every clash, cap and restriction is a flag.
  • It does not invent a distance or a fleet size for a route that has not been routed for the vehicle.
